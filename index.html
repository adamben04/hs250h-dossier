1Start Here — This Week

Two free tests decide whether this was a $2,300 win or a lesson. Do them first.

⛔ Test 1 — Brake Actuator Leak-Down (5 min, free) Cold start. Idle in Park. Foot off the brake. Time the pump cycles for 5 minutes.
Normal: one cycle every several minutes.   Failing: a cycle every 5–15 seconds.
No C1391 code does not clear it — the code appears late. This is the single biggest cost fork on the car.
⚠ Test 2 — Oil Consumption Baseline (free) Lexus documented this engine 2.5 qt low on a 4.5 qt fill at 97,256 mi. The 2AZ-FXE ring-drain defect is proven on this specific car.
Check the dipstick every 1,000–2,000 miles. Drain oil every 3,000. Treat oil as a consumable, not maintenance.

9Washington Legal Checklist

Hard deadlines — miss the first one and it gets expensive.

  • Verify the physical title. Sabyrzhan's legal name must be printed on the front. If it's signed over from his brother or a third party, that's title jumping and it becomes your problem.
  • Release of Interest. He must sign and print his name on that line.
  • Bill of Sale (TD-420-065). Both parties sign. Note the condition: "rebuilt title, damaged hood, peeling paint" so the Dept. of Revenue doesn't tax you at full book value.
  • Plates. Seller removes theirs under RCW 46.16A.200. Buy a $36 3-Day Trip Permit to drive home.
  • 15 calendar days to transfer at a King County subagency. Late fees run $50–125.
⚠ Rebuilt title — real-world consequences Expect higher insurance premiums and possible refusal from some carriers, sharply lower resale value, and some buyers who won't touch it. Also: the WA State Patrol rebuilt inspection (RCW 46.12.560) only verifies VIN stamps and parts receipts to prevent theft — it does not test frame alignment, crashworthiness, airbag function, or brake hydraulics. A passed inspection means very little mechanically.
